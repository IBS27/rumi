Five directions for the rumi interface

Each direction shows the same three screens: the start screen, the room review after a scan, and the design workspace once the agent has proposed products. Content is the sample living room and sample catalogue. None of this is wired to the app.

Drafting

Rumi's substance is measured geometry, so the interface borrows the conventions of an architectural plan: section-cut walls, dimension lines with ticks, a half-metre grid under the drawing, and rules that separate data the way title-block cells do. The plan itself is the same drawing on every screen, with the selected object or the fit problem marked in red pencil. IBM Plex Sans is the only typeface. Ink #1C2B3A on grey-blue paper #E8EDF1 and sheet #F7F9FB; red pencil #D0441C is reserved for the selected object and warnings.

Showroom

A furniture showroom rather than a dashboard: plaster and undyed linen surfaces, pale oak and wool in the drawings, graphite text, and one deep green kept for the primary action and the selected item. Instrument Sans is the only typeface; room and product names share one larger size, everything else is body size with tabular figures. The room drawing and its list sit on a single linen surface, so the shopping list reads as part of the room rather than a sidebar next to a canvas. Rooms and products shown are sample data.

Instrument

Rumi as a precision editor: the 3D room is the whole window and everything else floats over it as compact graphite panels, lighter than the viewer so depth reads without shadows doing the work. One typeface, Geist, with tabular numerals so dimensions and prices line up in columns. Palette: void #141618, panel #1F2327, raised #282D32, warm-neutral text #E7E2D9, celadon #8FC9A8 for selection and new items, amber #D9A65A only for fit conflicts. Estimated dimensions are drawn with dashed edges in the viewer and named as estimated in every list.

Editorial

Rumi set like an interiors magazine: a wide left margin carries the room's name, its measurements, and the designer's letter, while the right field holds the plan and the objects. Fraunces does the names and numbers; Instrument Sans does controls and captions. Paper white, ink, and one moss green used only where something can be clicked. Nothing is ruled off; space does the separating.

Plaster

Plaster: the room view is the whole window and the scan list, conversation and shopping list float over it as tinted plaster panels, lifted by one soft shadow. The only saturated colour is a deep teal for actions. Bricolage Grotesque for headings and DM Sans for everything else. Chalk white, eucalyptus, warm stone, dusty blue and a pale blush carry the panels; estimated dimensions are marked in ochre so they never look confirmed. The start screen is a drawn room outline you can drop a scan on.
